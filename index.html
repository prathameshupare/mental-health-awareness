UNDERSTANDING MENTAL HEALTH

What is mental health?

Mental health refers to our emotional, psychological and social wellbeing. It can influence how we think, feel, behave, handle stress and connect with others.

Just like physical health, mental health can change throughout life. Everyone can experience difficult emotions or periods of stress, and asking for support is a normal part of taking care of ourselves.

“

Taking care of your mental health is not a sign of weakness. It is part of taking care of yourself.

KNOWLEDGE IS A FIRST STEP

Common mental health challenges

Mental health experiences can be different for everyone. Learning about common challenges can reduce stigma and encourage people to seek appropriate support.

01
🌧️

Stress

Stress can occur when we feel unable to cope with demands or pressures. Learning healthy coping strategies can help.

02
🌊

Anxiety

Anxiety can involve persistent worry, fear or nervousness. When it becomes difficult to manage, professional support may be helpful.

03
🌱

Low Mood

Periods of sadness or low mood can happen to anyone. If these feelings persist or interfere with daily life, talking to a professional can help.

04
🔥

Burnout

Long periods of excessive pressure can leave a person feeling emotionally and physically exhausted.

05
🏠

Loneliness

Feeling disconnected from others can affect emotional wellbeing. Meaningful social connections can provide valuable support.

06
📚

Academic Pressure

Students may experience pressure related to studies, exams, expectations and future plans.

PAY ATTENTION

Recognising when someone may need support

Changes in behaviour or mood can sometimes indicate that someone is going through a difficult period. One sign alone does not diagnose a mental health condition.

✓ Noticeable changes in mood
✓ Withdrawing from usual activities
✓ Difficulty concentrating
✓ Changes in sleep or daily routine
✓ Feeling overwhelmed for a long period
✓ Difficulty managing everyday responsibilities

SMALL STEPS MATTER

Healthy habits for wellbeing

Self-care does not replace professional help, but healthy everyday habits can support overall wellbeing.

😴

Sleep

Maintain a consistent sleep routine and give your body enough time to rest.

🚶

Movement

Regular physical activity such as walking can support physical and mental wellbeing.

🥗

Nutrition

Regular, balanced meals and adequate hydration support overall health.

📵

Digital Breaks

Taking occasional breaks from screens can create space for rest and offline activities.

👥

Connection

Spend time with people you trust and maintain meaningful social connections.

🎨

Enjoyment

Make time for hobbies and activities that help you relax and recharge.

YOU DON'T HAVE TO HANDLE EVERYTHING ALONE

How to support someone

01

Listen

Give them your attention without immediately trying to solve the problem.

02

Show empathy

Let them know that their feelings matter and that they are not being judged.

03

Encourage help

Encourage them to speak with a trusted person or qualified mental health professional when appropriate.

04

Stay connected

A simple message or check-in can remind someone that they are not alone.

BREAKING THE STIGMA

Myths vs facts

MYTH

"Talking about mental health makes things worse."

FACT

Open and supportive conversations can make it easier for people to seek appropriate help.

MYTH

"Asking for help means you are weak."

FACT

Seeking support is a normal part of taking care of your wellbeing.

MYTH

"Mental health problems are always obvious."

FACT

People may experience difficulties that are not immediately visible to others.
